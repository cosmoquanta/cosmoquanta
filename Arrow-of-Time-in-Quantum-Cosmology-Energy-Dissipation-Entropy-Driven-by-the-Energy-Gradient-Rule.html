Arrow of Time in Quantum Cosmology: Energy Dissipation Entropy Driven by the Energy-Gradient Rule

Energy Dissipation Arrow of Time in Quantum Cosmology

Abstract

Building upon the analytic quantum thermodynamic framework I previously established (Zou, 2025), this article presents a discrete ontological model in which the arrow of time emerges from irreversible energy transfers driven by local energy gradients on a topologically fixed spatial network, with the temporal sequence identified as the successive updates of spatial energy redistribution. A multiplicative entropy, defined as the product of all node energies, increases exactly with every transfer, providing a non-statistical, step-by-step measure of temporal progression. I argue that energy dissipation is fundamentally identical to decoherence: the former supplies the physical mechanism, the latter the observational signature. This framework synthesizes three intellectual traditions—computational time (Wolfram, 't Hooft), gradient-driven thermodynamics (Annila), and decoherence (Joos & Zeh, Kiefer)—into a unified picture where time is the counting of irreversible energy redistribution on discrete space.

0.Introduction

This article builds upon the analytic quantum thermodynamic framework I previously established (Zou, 2025), which grounds time, entropy, and causality in a discrete elastic spacetime network of Space Elementary Quanta (SEQ). The arrow of time, in this view, emerges from irreversible energy redistribution driven by local energy gradients—a counting process of discrete state transitions that yields a computable, non-statistical multiplicative entropy.

This perspective aligns closely with the computational view of time advanced by Wolfram (2024) and 't Hooft (2018), who independently argue that time is not a fundamental coordinate but a sequential counting of elementary computational steps or state transformations in an underlying discrete substrate. It also converges with the gradient-driven evolution framework developed by Annila and collaborators (Tuisku et al., 2009; Annila, 2025), which identifies energy gradients as the universal driving force behind both quantum processes and cosmic evolution, with the Second Law manifesting as the natural tendency of energy to homogenize.

Crucially, the energy-dissipation arrow of time proposed here is not a rejection but a complementary interpretation of the decoherence-based arrow (Joos & Zeh, 1985; Kiefer et al., 2024, 2021). I argue that the process of energy dissipation is, at its core, identical to the process of decoherence. In the present ontology, decoherence and environment-induced superselection are understood as the macroscopic manifestations—observable through the lens of coarse-grained measurements—of the same underlying gradient-driven energy dissipation at the Planck scale. The two arrows are not competing mechanisms but two descriptions of the same process: one dynamical and microscopic (energy flow down gradients), the other informational and phenomenological (loss of phase coherence through environmental entanglement). The former provides the physical mechanism; the latter provides the operational signature.

Thus, this work synthesizes three intellectual traditions—computational time (Wolfram, 't Hooft), gradient-driven thermodynamics (Annila), and decoherence (Joos & Zeh, Kiefer)—into a unified ontological picture where the arrow of time is a direct, exact, and irreversible consequence of energy redistribution on a discrete spatial network, observed from complementary theoretical perspectives.

1. The Problem of Time in Quantum Physics

In the standard formulation of quantum mechanics, time enters as a continuous, external parameter. The Schrödinger equation is deterministic and timereversible: given the state at time t₀, the state at any future or past time is uniquely determined. No fundamental distinction exists between the forward and backward temporal directions. Yet our macroscopic experience is overwhelmingly asymmetric—we remember the past but not the future, we age, and energy distributions spontaneously homogenize. This conflict between microscopic reversibility and macroscopic irreversibility has been a central puzzle since Boltzmann's pioneering work on statistical entropy.

The conventional resolution appeals to statistical mechanics: entropy is a measure of the number of microscopic configurations corresponding to a given macroscopic state. The Second Law is understood as a probabilistic tendency rather than an absolute law: isolated systems evolve toward states of higher probability, but fluctuations that decrease entropy are not strictly forbidden. While this framework has been enormously successful, it suffers from several conceptual limitations. First, statistical entropy is inherently coarsegrained; it discards microscopic information and cannot track the detailed dynamical trajectory of a system. Second, the probabilistic interpretation of the Second Law leaves open the possibility of entropydecreasing fluctuations, which have never been observed in closed macroscopic systems. Third, it provides no direct, mechanistic connection between entropy increase and the flow of time itself.

The EnergyGradient Graph Automaton (EGG) model, as presented in the first three chapters of the foundational paper, offers an alternative ontology that addresses these limitations. By postulating a discrete, topologically fixed spatial network of energycarrying nodes, and a local energytransfer rule driven by energy gradients, the model constructs time and entropy from the ground up. The arrow of time is not an emergent approximation but an exact, stepbystep feature of the underlying dynamics.

2. The Ontological Substrate: Space Elementary Quanta (SEQ)

2.1 The Topologically Fixed Network: The EGG model begins with a fundamental postulate: the universe consists of a finite number of Space Elementary Quanta (SEQ) arranged in a threedimensional, topologically homeomorphic network. The adjacency relations between SEQ—which nodes are neighbors—are fixed and invariant throughout the entire evolution of the universe. This topological invariance is not an arbitrary assumption but is motivated by empirical and theoretical considerations. From a phenomenological perspective, observations of light bending around massive objects such as black holes reveal smooth spatial curvature without evidence of topological tearing or discontinuities. The gravitational field remains welldefined, and the connectivity structure of spacetime appears stable even under extreme conditions. From a theoretical standpoint, the validity of the Feynman path integral method presupposes the existence of a welldefined measure on the space of paths. If the topology of space were to change dynamically—altering adjacency relations and the set of possible paths—the path integral would become illdefined. Since the path integral has been extensively validated in quantum field theory, it is reasonable to infer that the underlying spacetime background operates within a stable topological class. Thus, the EGG model posits that the connectivity graph of SEQ is fixed. All motion, change, and physical processes correspond to the redistribution of energy across this immutable network. This builtin topological invariance provides an intuitive physical picture for the constancy of the speed of light, offers a concrete mechanism for global energy conservation, and simplifies the mathematical formulation of the model.

2.2 The TwoLayer Structure: SubPlanckian Substrate and SEQ: Each SEQ is not a primitive, pointlike entity. Rather, it is a topologically stable structure formed from a deeper, subPlanckian elastic substrate. The subPlanckian layer consists of elastic components that mediate interactions between SEQ, much like springs connecting nodes in a mechanical network. The SEQ themselves emerge as stable excitations or "knots" within this elastic medium. This twolayer structure is crucial for resolving a longstanding tension between general relativity and quantum field theory. In general relativity, spacetime is a smooth, continuous geometric manifold; in quantum field theory, matter fields are defined on a fiber bundle over spacetime, carrying spin and gauge degrees of freedom. The two formalisms appear incompatible because they rely on fundamentally different mathematical structures. However, within the EGG model, the subPlanckian elastic substrate provides a common foundation: the spin of SEQ arises from torsional elastic waves propagating on the substrate, while the elastic deformations of the network correspond to the geometric degrees of freedom of general relativity. Because spin and elasticity are fundamentally different types of excitations on the same substrate, they can coexist without interfering—especially in lowenergy states. This decoupling mechanism allows the model to simultaneously support the metric field of general relativity and the gauge fields of quantum field theory.

2.3 Energy Quantization and Ground State: Each SEQ carries an integer amount of energy, denoted mᵢ, measured in units of Planck's constant h. Thus, the energy of the ith SEQ is given by Eᵢ = mᵢ h , where mᵢ ∈ ℕ (positive integers). The total energy of a closed system is conserved: E_total = Σᵢ mᵢ h = constant . This quantization is not merely a mathematical convenience but reflects the fundamental discreteness of the model. Energy can only exist in integer multiples of h, and transfers occur only in discrete jumps of one quantum. Crucially, the ground state of each SEQ carries at least one unit of energy: mᵢ ≥ 1. This nonvanishing groundstate energy has significant implications. It provides a natural candidate for the cosmological constant in general relativity, and if the groundstate spin chirality of SEQ is fixed, it may explain the observed violation of parity in weak interactions. The fixed chirality also plays a central role in the model's interpretation of the Higgs mechanism and the scarcity of antimatter.

2.4 Elastic Bonds and Spacing Modulation: Adjacent SEQ are interconnected by elastic bonds—the subPlanckian springs mentioned above. These bonds maintain a dynamic equilibrium spacing under normal conditions. The bonds can be compressed or stretched by energy redistributions, and these deformations store elastic potential energy. The elastic response of the network is nonlinear and asymmetric: compression and stretching have different elastic coefficients, reflecting the chiral nature of the substrate. Importantly, the subPlanckian elastic components impose an upper bound on the degree of spacing modulation and tension between adjacent SEQ. This fundamental limit ensures that extreme deformations—such as those near black hole singularities—cannot tear the topological fabric of the network. Thus, the model naturally resolves the singularity problem of general relativity without invoking exotic physics. The harmonic oscillation period of SEQ is an integer multiple of Planck time tₚ. Consequently, all dynamical processes—including elastic strain interactions, harmonic conduction, and spinor field transmissions—are fundamentally constrained by discrete Plancktime intervals. This property ensures consistency with the discretetime hypotheses in quantum gravity theories and provides a natural cutoff for ultraviolet divergences.

2.5 Isotropy and Lorentz Covariance Any discrete model of spacetime must confront the challenge of restoring spatial isotropy so as to be compatible with the Lorentzcovariant rules established by observation. The EGG model addresses this in two ways. First, the topological network may contain primordial periodic or random dislocations that ensure physical isotropy. These defects are frozen in from the birth of the universe and preserve the overall 3D homeomorphism. Second, because the subPlanckian constituents are far smaller than the Planck scale, their statistical distribution can be tuned so that, within observational precision, the resulting dispersion relation is effectively Lorentzcovariant. Thus, macroscopic isotropy emerges naturally and remains consistent with all current experimental data.

3. Energy Transfer Dynamics: The Gradient Rule

3.1 The Fundamental Driving Force: The dynamics of the EGG model are governed by a single, local rule: energy flows from higherenergy SEQ to adjacent lowerenergy SEQ. Specifically, an energy transfer can occur between neighboring nodes i and j if and only if mᵢ > mⱼ + 1 . When this condition is satisfied, one quantum of energy (Planck's constant h) is transferred: mᵢ → mᵢ − 1 , mⱼ → mⱼ + 1 . This gradientdriven update rule is the microscopic realization of the Second Law of Thermodynamics. It ensures that energy spontaneously flows downhill along the steepest available gradient, homogenizing the energy distribution over time. The rule is irreversible: once a transfer has occurred, the reverse transfer (from j back to i) is forbidden unless the energy gradient later reverses due to subsequent transfers involving other neighbors. This local rule serves as the underlying mechanism for various gradient drives in traditional physics. Under appropriate approximation conditions, it reduces to: the potential energy gradient driving Newtonian mechanics, the temperature gradient driving thermodynamics, the metric curvature gradient driving general relativity, the electric potential gradient driving electrodynamics. Thus, the EGG model provides a unified, bottomup foundation for all known forces, rooted in the simple principle that energy seeks uniformity.

3.1.1 The Illusion of Reversibility: Why All Quantum Processes Are Irreversible: A potential objection to the gradientdriven dynamics is the apparent reversibility of quantum processes. Quantum optics experiments routinely demonstrate coherent superposition, quantum interference, and even timereversallike operations such as spinecho or photonecho. Do these not contradict the claim that all dynamics are fundamentally dissipative and gradientdriven? The answer lies in carefully delineating the system boundary. In every experiment that exhibits coherent quantum behavior—whether it is a trapped ion, a superconducting qubit, a Bose–Einstein condensate, or an optical interferometer—the quantum system of interest is never truly closed. It is maintained in its lowentropy, coherent state by an elaborate external apparatus: lasers provide cooling and trapping; magnetic fields confine and manipulate; radiofrequency drives control spin states; cryogenic refrigerators remove heat; and precision vacuum systems isolate the system from environmental decoherence. Each of these external devices consumes energy and dissipates heat into the broader environment. When the entire closed system—quantum sample plus all supporting apparatus plus the surrounding thermal bath—is considered, the net evolution is overwhelmingly dissipative. Energy flows from highgradient sources (electrical power, chemical batteries, laser pumps) into lowgradient sinks (the laboratory environment, ultimately the cosmic microwave background). The apparent reversibility of the quantum subsystem is a temporary, locally sustained excursion sustained by a continuous influx of free energy. It is the thermodynamic equivalent of a carefully balanced pencil standing on its tip—stable only as long as external constraints are actively maintained, but thermodynamically unstable and ultimately irreversible when the full system is accounted for. This perspective aligns with a fundamental fact: no empirical evidence supports the spontaneous reversible evolution of any closed physical system—whether classical, quantum, or cosmological—without external intervention. All observed dynamical processes, when their complete boundary conditions are properly accounted for, are thermodynamically irreversible. Electrons in atomic orbitals continuously radiate; the groundstate electron is an idealization, not a reality. Superconducting currents decay unless refrigerated. Quantum states decohere unless continuously corrected by errorcorrection protocols that themselves consume energy and generate entropy. There is no known phenomenon that supports a truly reversible, spontaneous return to a past state in a closed system. Therefore, the unitary evolution postulate of quantum mechanics—the assumption that quantum states evolve reversibly under the Schrödinger equation—is not a fundamental law of nature but an approximation valid only for carefully isolated subsystems over short time scales. The genuine, foundational dynamics is the gradientdriven, entropyincreasing energy redistribution described by the EGG model. In this view, quantum coherence is not a violation of the Second Law but a metastable pattern sustained by external freeenergy gradients—a special case of the general principle that energy flows from higher to lower concentrations, never the reverse, unless work is supplied from outside. And when that external work is included in the system boundary, the irreversibility is restored. This argument is not merely philosophical; it is computationally concrete. In the EGG framework, any attempt to simulate a "reversible" quantum gate must include the energy cost of the controlling fields, the dissipation in the control electronics, and the heat dumped into the environment. When these are modeled as additional SEQ nodes with their own energy gradients, the net entropy of the total closed system increases with every operation. The apparent reversibility of the logical qubit is a local, transient phenomenon—a standing wave pattern in a river of entropy production.

3.1.2 The Identity of Energy Dissipation and Decoherence: Two Sides of the Same Process: The decoherence program, pioneered by Joos and Zeh (1985) and developed by Kiefer and collaborators (2024, 2021), has established that classical properties emerge from the irreversible loss of quantum phase coherence through interaction with the environment. This is an informational account: it describes how interference is suppressed and how a preferred basis is selected, without explaining why this process is thermodynamically unavoidable. The energy-dissipation perspective supplies exactly that missing explanation. When energy flows down local gradients from higher to lower concentrations, it redistributes itself irreversibly across the spatial network. This flow, at the microscopic level, is the physical process that generates entropy. The loss of quantum phase relations is not a separate phenomenon; it is the informational signature of that same energy redistribution. Every elementary energy exchange between a system and its environment is simultaneously a step of dissipation and a step of decoherence. Thus, the two frameworks are not competing. Energy dissipation provides the physical mechanism: energy moves from higher to lower gradients, increasing entropy. Decoherence provides the observable consequence: phase relations decay, interference fades, and classical behavior emerges. One is the dynamical cause; the other is the measured effect. They describe the same underlying process from complementary perspectives — one thermodynamic, the other informational. In this view, the arrow of time is one, not two.
